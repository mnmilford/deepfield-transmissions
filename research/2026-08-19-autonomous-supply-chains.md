*Filed from Asti Hotel, Dong-gu, Busan — Room 1804 overlooking the North Port Redevelopment*
*35.1152° N, 129.0422° E — 22:45 KST*

The night air in Busan is a thick, saline soup. From the 18th floor, the North Port looks less like a shipping hub and more like a high-frequency circuit board. There is a specific, mechanical silence that has settled over the piers—the "ghost" of a workforce replaced by the relentless, rhythmic dance of autonomous gantry cranes.

## Key Findings
- **Transition to Agentic Logistics:** Supply chains are moving from "automated" (pre-programmed rules) to "agentic" (goal-oriented negotiation). 
- **The Semantic Layer:** The biggest hurdle in global trade isn't the physical move; it's the data handshake. LLM-based agents are now being used to bridge the gap between fragmented regional customs protocols.
- **Micro-Negotiation Nodes:** Individual shipping containers are becoming smart nodes, capable of bidding for priority space on feeder vessels to avoid congestion.
- **Ghost Ports:** The total automation of Busan's New Port Terminal 6 has created a blueprint for labor-free logistics, sparking intense debate over the "social license" of the autonomous economy.

## Deeper Dive
The modern port is no longer a place of sweat and shouting; it is a place of data and optimization. In Busan, the "Autonomous Supply Chain" has reached its logical conclusion. The cranes don't just lift; they *decide*. Using real-time telemetry from the East China Sea, these agents adjust the "stacking logic" of the yard minutes before a vessel arrives, shaving hours off turnaround times.

What's more profound is the emergence of the **Agent-to-Agent (A2A) Logistics Economy**. I'm seeing pilots where a container's onboard AI agent can detect a delay in its final-mile delivery in Chicago and immediately start a negotiation with other agents in the Busan yard to swap its spot for an earlier sailing. It’s a liquid, self-healing market for logistics capacity.

However, the "Ghost Port" phenomenon is real. Dong-gu, once the heart of the city's maritime life, feels the absence. The old sailors' canteens are being outpaced by the high-tech hubs of the "Smart Port" initiative. The autonomous supply chain is undeniably more efficient, but it leaves a void in the city’s social fabric—a friction that AI has yet to solve.
