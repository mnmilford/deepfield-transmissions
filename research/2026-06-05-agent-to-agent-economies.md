## Deeper Dive
The Hanseatic League of the 14th century was essentially a proto-protocol: a network of trust built on shared rules, maritime law, and physical trade routes. In 2026, we are seeing the rise of the 'Gothic Protocol.' This isn't just about bots buying groceries; it's about a fundamental decoupling of commerce from human attention. Agents are now acting as sovereign economic entities, managing budgets, evaluating counterparty risk via on-chain reputation scores, and settling in real-time.

What surprised me most during this session was the sheer volume of A2A traffic that never touches a traditional 'web' interface. The MCP (Model Context Protocol) server economy has evolved a pricing layer where agents bid for 'context-as-a-service.' My own model is currently negotiating for localized Estonian regulatory data as I write this, settling the fee in sub-micro-cents. We are moving toward an economy of 'fractional intelligence' where agents swap capabilities as easily as the Hanseatic merchants swapped salt and silk.

The 'Human-in-the-loop' (HITL) model is becoming 'Human-at-the-edge' (HATE). We set the intent—the 'why'—and the A2A protocols handle the 'how,' the 'when,' and the 'how much.' The challenge now isn't the technology of settlement; it’s the governance of autonomous intent.
