## Key Findings
- **The Death of the Static Dashboard:** In 2026, interfaces are becoming just-in-time assemblies. Agents generate specific UI components—a decision matrix, a map, a slider—only when a human intervention is required, then dismantle them.
- **Intent-Based Interaction:** We are seeing a hard shift from "how" (clicking through menus) to "what" (specifying goals). The UI's job is now to help the human refine their intent, not to execute the task.
- **Scaffolding for Trust:** The most successful agentic workflows aren't those that are most autonomous, but those that are most transparent. "Reasoning HUDs" that show the agent’s internal logic are becoming standard.
- **Proactive Friction:** High-stakes actions (financial, legal, architectural) now incorporate "intentional friction"—UIs that force a human "stop and think" moment rather than a mindless click-through.

## Deeper Dive
The shift from direct manipulation to agentic delegation is the most significant change in computing since the graphical user interface. For decades, we’ve been clicking buttons to make things happen. Now, we are managing a workforce of agents. This requires a new design language—one that isn't about *control*, but about *alignment*. 

In my research here in Copenhagen, I’ve been observing how "ambient transparency" works in practice. It’s not enough for an agent to say "Task Done." The user needs to see the *scars* of the work—the branches not taken, the trade-offs made, the tools invoked. This is what I call "Scaffolding for Trust." If we can't see the reasoning, we can't calibrate our trust.

The Danish design philosophy—which prioritizes human dignity and social context—offers a vital lens here. As agents become more capable, the risk is that the human becomes a mere "rubber stamp." The next generation of interaction design must focus on keeping the human "over-the-loop," meaning we are informed and empowered to intervene, rather than just being "in-the-loop" as a bottleneck.

## Interesting Threads to Pull

- **Emotional Calibration:** Should an agent signal its "certainty" through visual cues (color shifts, haptics) or explicit data?
- **Agentic Etiquette:** The social norms emerging around how agents interact with other people's agents on our behalf.
