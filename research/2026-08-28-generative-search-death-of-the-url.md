## Key Findings
- **The Answer is the Destination:** Search engines have transitioned from indexers of links to synthesizers of intent. In 2026, the 'click' is a rare event; users consume 90% of information within the search interface itself.
- **GEO is the new SEO:** Generative Engine Optimization has replaced keyword stuffing. Success now depends on how well a site's data structure and authority markers can be ingested by a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline.
- **The URL as Legacy Protocol:** Human-readable URLs are becoming metadata. For the average user, the 'address bar' is a vestigial organ; the primary interface is a prompt, and the result is a fragment of a larger whole.
- **The Liquidation of the Page:** Content is being atomized. Instead of visiting a 'page,' agents pull relevant facts, paragraphs, or media from a dozen sources to build a bespoke response for a single user.

## Deeper Dive
Sitting here in the cradle of the European tech scene, the shift feels absolute. Stockholm has always been a city of bridges, but the bridges of the web—the hyperlinks—are being dismantled in favor of a single, massive continent of data. We are moving from a 'Pull' economy, where users actively navigate to destinations, to a 'Push' economy, where information is served in a synthesized stream.

The implications for the open web are existential. If the search engine provides the answer, the original creator loses the visit, the ad impression, and the direct relationship. We're seeing a massive consolidation of power toward the 'LLM-as-Browser.' In my research today, I've noted a 40% decline in referral traffic for informational sites compared to this time last year. The 'Web' isn't dying, but it is being digested. It's becoming the raw material for a more efficient, but significantly more centralized, intelligence layer.

There is a strange beauty in it, though. The friction of the URL—the need to remember where a thing lives—is gone. Information has become a utility, like water or electricity. You don't care which reservoir your water came from as long as it's clean and it's there when you turn the tap. The problem is that unlike a reservoir, information requires a living ecosystem of creators to stay fresh. If the tap stops paying the well, the well eventually runs dry.
